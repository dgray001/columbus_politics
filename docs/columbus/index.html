Columbus is a home-rule municipality: under Article XVIII, Section 3 of the Ohio Constitution1, Ohio cities and villages may exercise “all powers of local self-government,” and Columbus does so through its own city charter rather than relying solely on state statute. This page introduces how Columbus city government is organized; the sections below cover the city's executives, its legislative council, its administrative departments and boards, and how city elections work.

Columbus's charter was adopted by the city's electors on May 5, 19142, and took effect January 1, 19163, replacing the city's earlier state-statute-based government with home rule. The charter has been amended many times since, most notably in 20184, when voters approved expanding city council from seven members elected entirely at-large to nine members, each tied to a residential district but still elected citywide; that structure was phased in starting with the 2023 general election and took full effect January 1, 20245.

Citywide Executives

Columbus elects three separate citywide executive officers: the mayor, the city attorney, and the city auditor. Each is a distinct chartered office serving a four-year term.

City Council

City council is Columbus's legislative branch: nine members, each tied to a residential district but elected by voters citywide, serving staggered four-year terms.

Administration

Day-to-day city operations run through mayoral departments, boards and commissions, the Civil Service Commission, and the city's budget and revenue system.

Elections

Covers how Columbus's candidate elections work, along with the charter's separate process for citizen-initiated ordinances, referendums, and recalls.
