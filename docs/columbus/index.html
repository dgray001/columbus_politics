Columbus is a home-rule municipality: under Article XVIII, Section 3 of the Ohio Constitution,[1] Ohio cities and villages may exercise “all powers of local self-government,” and Columbus does so through its own city charter rather than relying solely on state statute. This page introduces how Columbus city government is organized; the sections below cover the city's executives, its legislative council, its administrative departments and boards, and how city elections work.

Columbus's charter was adopted by the city's electors on May 5, 1914,[2] and took effect January 1, 1916,[3] replacing the city's earlier state-statute-based government with home rule. The charter is amended frequently by Columbus voters. Proposed amendments can be placed on the ballot directly by the City Council or through citizen-led initiative petitions.[4] Additionally, the city convenes a Charter Review Commission[5] every ten years to evaluate the document and recommend comprehensive updates.

Elected Executives

Columbus elects three separate city-wide executive officers: the mayor, the city attorney, and the city auditor. Each is a distinct chartered office serving a four-year term.

City Council

City council is Columbus's legislative branch: nine members, each tied to a residential district but elected by voters citywide, serving staggered four-year terms.

Administration

Day-to-day city operations run through mayoral departments, boards and commissions, the Civil Service Commission, and the city's budget and revenue system.
