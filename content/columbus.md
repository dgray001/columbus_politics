Columbus is a home-rule municipality: under [Article XVIII, Section 3 of the Ohio Constitution](cite:oh_const_art18_sec3), Ohio cities and villages may exercise “all powers of local self-government,” and Columbus does so through its own city [charter](resource:charter) rather than relying solely on state statute. This page introduces how Columbus city government is organized; the sections below cover the city's executives, its legislative council, its administrative departments and boards, and how city elections work.

Columbus's charter was adopted by the city's electors on [May 5, 1914](cite:charter_adoption), and took effect [January 1, 1916](cite:charter_sec235), replacing the city's earlier state-statute-based government with home rule. The charter is amended frequently by Columbus voters. [Proposed amendments can be placed on the ballot directly by the City Council or through citizen-led initiative petitions](cite:charter_sec45). Additionally, the city convenes a [Charter Review Commission](cite:charter_sec236) every ten years to evaluate the document and recommend comprehensive updates.

## [Citywide Executives](/columbus/citywide-executives)

Columbus elects three separate citywide executive officers: the mayor, the city attorney, and the city auditor. Each is a distinct chartered office serving a four-year term.

## [City Council](/columbus/city-council)

City council is Columbus's legislative branch: nine members, each tied to a residential district but elected by voters citywide, serving staggered four-year terms.

## [Administration](/columbus/administration)

Day-to-day city operations run through mayoral departments, boards and commissions, the Civil Service Commission, and the city's budget and revenue system.

## [Elections](/columbus/elections)

Covers how Columbus's candidate elections work, along with the charter's separate process for citizen-initiated ordinances, referendums, and recalls.
