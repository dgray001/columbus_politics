Native settlements, including Mingo, Shawnee, Wyandot, and Delaware villages, occupied the confluence of the Scioto and Olentangy rivers before Lucas Sullivant founded [Franklinton](cite:franklinton_founding_1797) on its west bank in 1797. Ohio's legislature chose the opposite bank in [1812](cite:columbus_founding_1812) to found Columbus as a new, centrally located state capital, a status it assumed in 1816 alongside its incorporation as a borough. It was [chartered as a city in 1834](cite:columbus_charter_1834), and for the next eighty years its powers were defined piecemeal by further acts of the state legislature.

Columbus is a home-rule municipality: under [Article XVIII, Section 3 of the Ohio Constitution](cite:oh_const_art18_sec3), Ohio cities and villages may exercise “all powers of local self-government,” and Columbus does so through its own city [charter](resource:charter) rather than relying solely on state statute. This page introduces how Columbus city government is organized; the sections below cover the city's executives, its legislative council, its administrative departments and boards, and how city elections work.

Columbus's charter was adopted by the city's electors on [May 5, 1914](cite:charter_adoption), and took effect [January 1, 1916](cite:charter_sec235), replacing the city's earlier state-statute-based government with home rule. The charter is amended frequently by Columbus voters. [Proposed amendments can be placed on the ballot directly by the City Council or through citizen-led initiative petitions](cite:charter_sec45). Additionally, the city convenes a [Charter Review Commission](cite:charter_sec236) every ten years to evaluate the document and recommend comprehensive updates.

## [Elected Executives](/columbus/elected-executives)

Columbus elects three separate citywide executive officers: the mayor, the city attorney, and the city auditor. Each holds a distinct chartered office and serves a four-year term. If the mayor's office becomes vacant, the council president succeeds automatically; vacancies in the other two offices are instead filled by council appointment.

## [City Council](/columbus/city-council)

City council is Columbus's legislative branch, responsible for local ordinances and financial oversight. It consists of nine members, each tied to a residential district but elected by voters citywide to staggered four-year terms.

## [Administration](/columbus/administration)

Day-to-day municipal operations are carried out by executive departments under the mayor's direction, alongside the smaller independent offices of the city attorney and auditor. The administration also relies on specialized boards, the independent Civil Service Commission, and the city's financial and revenue systems.

## [Elections](/columbus/elections)

The city charter establishes rules and procedures for all local elections. It also reserves direct democratic powers for voters by allowing for citizen-initiated ordinances, referendums, and the recall of elected officials.
