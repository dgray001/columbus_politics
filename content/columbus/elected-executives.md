Columbus elects three separate citywide executive officers: the [mayor](cite:mayor_term_qualifications), the [city attorney](cite:attorney_term_qualifications), and the [city auditor](cite:auditor_term_qualifications). Each holds a distinct chartered office and none sits on the legislative council. All three are elected to four-year terms and must be electors of the city who maintain residence in Columbus throughout their term of office.

## The Mayor

The mayor is Columbus's chief executive, and the [charter vests the city's executive and administrative powers](cite:mayor_executive_powers) in the mayor together with the directors of departments and other administrative officers and boards it establishes.

### Powers and Duties

The mayor directly [appoints and can remove the directors of public safety and public service, along with the office's own executive staff](cite:mayor_appoint_officers). Other appointments require council's sign-off: the mayor names the members of the civil service commission, the board of health, and any advisory commissions council creates, but only [with council's concurrence](cite:mayor_appoint_concurrence), and removing them the same way requires written charges and a hearing before council.

Day to day, the charter casts the mayor as [chief conservator of the peace and general overseer of city government](cite:mayor_general_powers): supervising the administration of city affairs, including purchases and sales made on the city's behalf, enforcing ordinances, recommending measures to council, and keeping council informed of the city's financial condition through required reports. To coordinate that administration, the mayor also [convenes a cabinet of department directors and executive staff](cite:mayor_cabinet).

The mayor's oversight extends to an investigative power: [the affairs of any department, or the conduct of any officer or employee, can be examined without notice](cite:mayor_investigation), and anyone appointed to conduct such an examination can compel witnesses and documents on the same terms as council itself. Despite this executive authority, the mayor also has [a seat in council, without a vote, but with the right to introduce ordinances and take part in council's debates](cite:mayor_council_seat).

### Vacancy and Succession

When the mayor is temporarily absent from the city, the mayor may [designate a member of the mayor's cabinet as acting mayor](cite:mayor_acting), delegating whatever powers and duties are needed for the duration of the absence. A broader temporary inability, one that keeps the mayor from discharging the office's powers and duties at all, follows a stricter procedure: the mayor or the mayor's agent must [transmit a signed, written declaration to a designated cabinet member](cite:mayor_temporary_inability), setting out the reasons, and that cabinet member then serves as acting mayor for up to six months or until the office is vacated.

If ninety consecutive days pass with the mayor unable to discharge the office and no written declaration of temporary inability in effect, the inability becomes permanent as a matter of charter law. That lapse works a forfeiture of the office, and the [directors of public safety and public service must request that the appropriate official commence a quo warranto action to formally vacate it](cite:mayor_permanent_inability).

An actual vacancy, whether from death, resignation, removal, or disqualification, triggers automatic succession rather than appointment. The [council president succeeds to the office of mayor](cite:mayor_successor), ceasing to be a council member, and serves until the January 1 following the next regular municipal election. If that election is a regularly scheduled mayoral election, the winner then serves a full new four-year term; otherwise, the successor mayor serves out the remainder of the unexpired term.

## The City Attorney

The city attorney is [the city's legal adviser and attorney and counsel, for the city and all its officers and departments in matters relating to their official duties](cite:attorney_powers). Like the mayor and auditor, the office is a directly elected, independent citywide post, held by a licensed Ohio attorney.

### Powers and Duties

The city attorney is [the legal adviser of and attorney and counsel for the city, and for all officers and departments thereof in matters relating to their official duties](cite:attorney_powers). The office prosecutes or defends all suits on the city's behalf, prepares and certifies the city's contracts, bonds, and other written instruments, and may appoint a department deputy, along with such assistants, secretaries, and clerks as council authorizes; the deputy holds full power to perform the city attorney's duties.

The city attorney also serves as [prosecuting attorney of municipal court](cite:attorney_prosecuting), detailing assistants as needed and performing the same duties, so far as applicable, as the county prosecuting attorney. Beyond municipal court, the office [prosecutes or defends all complaints, suits, and controversies in which the city is a party](cite:attorney_representing), along with other matters relating to city affairs as directed by council resolution or ordinance.

Council, any department director, or any officer, board, or commission outside a department may [request the city attorney's written opinion on a question of law involving their respective powers or duties](cite:attorney_opinions).

### Vacancy and Succession

If the city attorney is [temporarily unable to discharge the powers and duties of the office](cite:attorney_temporary_inability), the city attorney or an agent must transmit a signed, written declaration to the deputy city attorney setting forth the reasons. The deputy city attorney then becomes acting city attorney for the duration of the inability, up to six months or until the office is vacated, whichever comes first.

If the inability lasts [ninety consecutive days without such a declaration in effect](cite:attorney_permanent_inability), it works a forfeiture of the office. In that case the deputy city attorney requests that the appropriate official commence a quo warranto action to vacate the office, and a successor is named once judgment is entered.

Unlike the mayor's office, which passes automatically to the council president, [a vacancy in the city attorney's office is filled by council appointment](cite:attorney_successor). The appointed successor serves until January 1 following the next regular municipal election: if that election is the regular election for city attorney, the successor serves only until then and a city attorney is elected to a full four-year term; otherwise the successor serves out the remainder of the unexpired term.

## The City Auditor

The auditor is the city's [chief accounting officer](cite:auditor_powers), keeping systemized records of the city's receipts, disbursements, assets, and liabilities and reporting on them for each fiscal year.

### Powers and Duties

As the city's [chief accounting officer](cite:auditor_powers), the auditor keeps a detailed, systematized record of the city's receipts, disbursements, assets, and liabilities, and periodically presents that record to officials and the public in summaries showing its effect on city finances, both citywide and by department, including each city-owned utility.

The auditor also sets the [accounting methods and reporting forms](cite:auditor_accounts_forms) that every city department must use, and [requires daily reports](cite:auditor_daily_reports) from each department on money received and how it was spent, while tracking appropriations made by council against actual and contracted expenditures.

At the close of each fiscal year, or more often if council asks, the auditor [audits the accounts of every city officer and department](cite:auditor_audit) and reports the findings to council; the same review applies whenever an officer leaves office, with any resulting debt to the city referred to the mayor and city attorney for collection. Before any payment leaves the treasury, the auditor must [draw a warrant](cite:auditor_claims) confirming the claim is lawfully due, covered by a council appropriation, and approved in writing by the relevant department head.

The auditor may also [appoint a deputy](cite:auditor_deputy) and other assistants and clerks as authorized by council; the deputy holds full power to perform the auditor's duties.

### Vacancy and Succession

If the auditor is temporarily unable to discharge the duties of office, the auditor or the auditor's agent transmits a signed, written declaration of the reasons to the deputy auditor, who becomes [acting auditor for the duration, up to six months, or until the office is vacated](cite:auditor_temporary_inability).

If that inability continues for ninety consecutive days without a written declaration in effect, the office is forfeited, and the [deputy auditor must request that the appropriate official commence a quo warranto action to vacate it](cite:auditor_permanent_inability).

Unlike the mayor's office, which passes automatically to the council president, an actual vacancy in the auditor's office is filled by council appointment, the same mechanism used for the city attorney. The [appointed successor serves until the following January 1st after the next regular municipal election](cite:auditor_successor): a full four-year term if that election is the regular election for auditor, or the remainder of the unexpired term otherwise.
